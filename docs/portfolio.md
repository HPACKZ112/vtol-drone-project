# Portfolio evidence

The README gives an interviewer an overview. The most useful supporting material
shows your reasoning and connects your own work to evidence. The initial scaffold
is a starting point; calculations from assumed inputs do not demonstrate a built
or flown aircraft.

## Build an evidence trail

| Contribution to document | Evidence to add | What to be ready to explain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Defined a mission and requirements | Reviewed requirements and revisions | Why the targets matter and how to verify them |
| Compared tilt-rotor and tilt-wing | Weighted trade study with sources | Trade-offs, uncertain scores, and reasons for the choice |
| Developed or reviewed sizing calculations | Equations, readable code, tests, and sensitivity results | Units, assumptions, physical trends, and model limits |
| Selected components | Sourced BOM and thrust/current data | Margins, compatibility, budget, and uncertainty |
| Designed structure or mechanisms | CAD, drawings, calculations, and revisions | Loads, material choice, assembly, and failure modes |
| Validated predictions | Raw measurements, processing scripts, and comparison plots | Calibration, repeats, error, and discrepancies |

## Before using the project in an application

- Add your name, course, role, dates, and actual contributions once confirmed.
- Record which code you wrote, adapted, or reviewed, and any assistance used.
- Add one clear photograph or CAD view when an actual design exists.
- Choose two or three results you can reproduce and discuss without the script.
- Make a short design report covering problem, method, evidence, and limitations.
- Ensure every performance claim points to a dated measurement or labelled model.

## Interview preparation

Explain one calculation by hand, one design trade-off, and one assumption that
needs measurement. Be able to show how a requirement connects to a decision,
calculation, and planned test. Discuss a real error or revision once you have one.
Use terms such as "estimated" or "modelled" for analytical results, and reserve
"measured", "built", or "flight-tested" for evidence that supports those claims.
